The missing channels: grammars, DSG, and DCS

A direct answer to “Does the 3 include frequency in grammars? DCS as well 4th?” — with the two absent channels actually measured, not just described.

No. The three channels were pc (explicitly written), an (inherited by anuvṛtti) and ad (supplied by a governing adhikāra). All three are structural positions inside the same 3,983 sūtras — they describe how a term is required in order to read one rule. They are not three corpora, so they cannot contain “frequency in grammars”.

DCS was not a fourth measured channel either. It entered the design only indirectly: DIFFICULTY_ORDERING_RESULT.md supplied the function-word-exclusion principle (τ = 0.887 inside content vocabulary). No DCS count appeared in core_terms.tsv.

So the right fix is not “3 + DCS = 4”. It is a two-axis design: keep the three structural channels on Axis A, then add grammatical-discourse frequency on Axis B and DCS as a register control on Axis C. DCS measures how often a word turns up in epic / Purāṇa / kāvya text, which is the wrong target for a technical term.

01What the three channels actually are

Axis A · structural demand

One text: the Aṣṭādhyāyī. Three ways a term is loaded: pc 13,068 occurrences / 6,741 forms · an 9,808 / 1,571 · ad 14,323 / 69 headings. 37,199 required-term readings, ~1.8 implicit for every 1 explicit.

Axis B · grammatical discourse

New here. Channel G: 12,627 passages of Mahābhāṣya, Kaumudī, Bālamanoramā, Vākyapadīya, Bhūṣaṇasāra, Phitsūtras. Channel L: mentions + citation density across 4,383 DSG entries.

Axis C · literary register

New here. Channel D: the DCS lemma ranking table (50,474 keys). Used to show what not to optimise for, and to flag terms that a student will never meet outside a grammar.

07Data quality: the HK → SLP1 fix and the ten short stems

DSG's column labelled slp1 is Harvard-Kyoto, not SLP1. DCS is keyed in SLP1, so joining the column directly produces wrong keys: Adeza → AdeSa, saJjJA → saYjYA, kRt → kft. HK and SLP1 differ by a cyclic substitution (R = ṛ ↔ f, G = ṅ ↔ N, N = ṇ ↔ R, z = ś ↔ S), so a single-pass longest-match converter is required — sequential replacement corrupts the result. The conversion is verified against DSG's independent IAST headword column, and every one of the 4,383 headwords is re-joined in dsg_dcs_join.tsv.

The class-nasal ↔ anusvāra variants are now generated in SLP1 as well as in Devanāgarī: aNga/aMga, saYjYA/saMjYA, saNKyA/saMKyA, saMbanDa/sambanDa. Word-final nasals are deliberately not varied — aM (the letter aṃ) and an are different words. Variants are alternative spellings of one lemma, so the best-attested spelling is used rather than summing counts (which would double-count); every attested variant is recorded in external_channels.tsv.

The ten short stems are measured, not declared unmeasurable

The first release left these ten blank because it, ca and na collide with English words in Abhyankar's English definitions. They are now measured with one documented rule in three independent fields. English prose is explicitly an upper bound; the Russian definitions (where English function words are absent) and the Devanāgarī headword index are collision controls. No value is guessed and no value is blank.

08Verdict — how to use each channel

Keep A as the backbone

pc / an / ad determine when a term becomes unavoidable while reading Pāṇini. This is what fixes batch order. Do not dilute it with corpus counts.

Use G to validate and to pace

Grammar-corpus df is the independent check that the tradition really talks about a term. It also tells you how much prose exposure a student gets in Mahābhāṣya vs. Kaumudī vs. Vākyapadīya.

Use L for the reference shelf

DSG entry length, citation density and cross-mentions decide which terms deserve a full dictionary entry in a student's notes — they are a salience signal, not a learn-first signal.

Use D as a negative control

Low or zero DCS count is evidence for teaching a term early: it will never be picked up by reading literature. High DCS count mostly flags operators and everyday words that must be excluded from the vocabulary list.

Limitations. G counts document frequency over passages, not raw sūtra-style readings; passage granularity differs by source (an āhnika chunk is longer than a kārikā). Matching reuses the curated prefix inventory from build_batches.py with class-nasal ↔ anusvāra variants in both Devanāgarī and SLP1. L measures all 119 terms in three fields (English prose, Russian prose, Devanāgarī headwords); short stems use exact-token matching and their known English collision is reported rather than suppressed. D is the locally pinned 50,474-key DCS ranking table, not the full 5,688,416-token / 90,349-lemma export; multi-part rows use their first lemma. DSG's mislabelled HK column is never joined to DCS directly: see slp1_keys.py, dsg_entries.tsv and dsg_dcs_join.tsv. Two DSG headwords (jihvāmūlīya / upadhmānīya) have non-Latin placeholder characters and yield no SLP1 key; they are flagged as slp1_source=none.
