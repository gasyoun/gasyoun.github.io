04 · mechanism

Guṇa and vṛddhi — the vowel gradation engine

This is the key to understanding how one root gives rise to an entire word family. Sanskrit recognizes three "grades" of the root vowel.

Three grades

GradeNameWhat happens
Zero(root as listed)Vowel not strengthened
Guṇaguṇa — "quality"First strengthening
Vṛddhivṛddhi — "growth"Full strengthening

Worked example: √budh → bodha → bauddha

budh — root (dhātu), "to wake, to realize." The same root appears in the word "Buddha" (buddha — "awakened one"). bodha — guṇa grade: u → o. Noun "awakening, realization." bauddha — vṛddhi grade: u → au. Adjective "Buddhist," i.e. "relating to one who is awakened."

Morphological chain: "to wake → awakening → Buddhist"

Sandhi rule before a following vowel

When a guṇa (o) or vṛddhi (au) grade appears before another vowel, sandhi applies:

o → av,  au → āv

Example: root √bhū (to be) + ending a → bhavati (he becomes / exists).

Other sandhi patterns (by root vowel):

  • a / ā: unchanged before a vowel; only length varies.
  • i / ī: e → ay, ai → āy (cf. √ji + a → jaya).
  • ṛ / ṝ: ar and ār stay unchanged (cf. √kṛ → karoti, kārya).
  • ḷ: al and āl stay unchanged; almost no roots attested.

06 · teaching notes

Five teaching takeaways

  • Sanskrit vocabulary is built, not borrowed. 1,576 compound entries and 816 roots dwarf 55 Sanskritized Middle-Indo-Aryan and 24 borrowed-back forms. The data describes a generative engine, not a received lexicon.
  • Uncertainty is part of the subject. 447 entries have unknown etymology — more than all vṛddhi derivatives (250). They sit beside 22 reconstructed (*) and 9 sound-symbolic terms.
  • Borrowing is wide but shallow. 50 donor-language buckets, but most are tiny. The largest origin families are 556 PIE root subcategories and 106 PIE word subcategories.
  • Do not read these as percentages of Sanskrit. These are Wiktionary's curated category counts at snapshot time, not a corpus survey. They show editorial shape — useful for teaching how taxonomies get built.
  • Avoid tree visualizations. The 42 names are overlapping tags, not a hierarchy. One word can be a compound, a vṛddhi derivative, and PIE-rooted simultaneously. Any visualization forcing a single tree misrepresents the data.
