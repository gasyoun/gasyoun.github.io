Why this exists. The parent category contains no word pages at all — only subcategories. Backing up the parent page alone would preserve 42 labels and none of the vocabulary. This snapshot therefore also captures every direct member one level down, so the taxonomy can be rebuilt from these files without Wiktionary.

03 · restoring

How to rebuild

  • raw/subcategories.json gives the authoritative list of 42 subcategory titles from the MediaWiki API.
  • raw/subcategory-members.json maps each of those titles to its direct subcategories and word pages (5,426 titles total). That is enough to reconstruct the tree one level down.
  • raw/category.html and raw/category-rendered.json preserve the page as displayed, including the introductory definitions and the (N c, M e) counts.
  • raw/cognates.json holds the member lists of 114 per-language PIE root/word categories and 4 inheritance layers used by the cognate map and timeline. Its source URLs are in cognates-manifest.json.
  • python3 wiki/scripts/fetch_snapshot.py --offline rebuilds every derived file from these raw bytes with no network, preserving the original timestamp and checksums — useful if Wiktionary is unreachable.
  • To refresh against a live Wiktionary, run python3 wiki/scripts/fetch_cognates.py then python3 wiki/scripts/fetch_snapshot.py; they rewrite the raw files, both manifests, SNAPSHOT.md and all four data files, and record a new timestamp and checksums on every run.
  • Verify any file with shasum -a 256 <file> against the digest in manifest.json.
