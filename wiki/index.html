04 · teaching notes

What to say in the classroom

  • Sanskrit vocabulary is overwhelmingly built, not borrowed. 1,576 compound entries and 816 roots dwarf the 55 Sanskritized Middle-Indo-Aryan and 24 borrowed-back forms. Students often assume a received, monolithic lexicon; the data describes a generative engine.
  • Uncertainty is part of the subject. 447 entries have unknown etymology — more than all vṛddhi derivatives — and sit beside 22 explicitly reconstructed (*) forms and 9 sound-symbolic ones.
  • Borrowing is wide but shallow. There are 50 donor-language buckets under "borrowed terms", yet most are tiny; the largest origin families are 556 Proto-Indo-European root subcategories and 106 PIE word subcategories.
  • Do not read these as percentages of Sanskrit. They are Wiktionary's curated category counts at snapshot time, not a corpus survey. They show editorial shape, which is exactly what makes them useful for teaching how taxonomies get built.
  • Avoid word clouds, force-directed graphs and sunbursts here. The 42 names are overlapping tags, not a hierarchy: one word can be a compound, a vṛddhi derivative and PIE-rooted at once. Any visual that forces a single tree misrepresents the data.
