03 · teaching notes

What to say in the classroom

  • Inheritance is a chain, not a single event. The source distinguishes Proto-Indo-European, Proto-Indo-Iranian, Proto-Indo-Aryan and Vedic Sanskrit inheritance. Ask students why a word can be described at several depths at once.
  • Most Sanskrit words are built, not inherited from a finished lexicon. The compound category and the root category are where the vocabulary is generated; the guṇa/vṛddhi lab shows the mechanism.
  • Contact leaves shallow but wide traces. Borrowing is spread over 50 donor-language buckets; calques translate rather than import. Neither behaves like inheritance.
  • Uncertainty is a layer too. Unknown etymologies outnumber vṛddhi derivatives. Put this on the timeline deliberately: not knowing is a documented state of the field, not a gap in the teaching material.
  • Do not turn the bars into percentages. These are editorial counts at snapshot time from a living wiki. Their value is showing the shape of a taxonomy, which is exactly the lesson.
