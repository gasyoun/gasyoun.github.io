Dhātu desk

Wiktionary files Sanskrit words under 42 etymology tags; earlier work in this workspace catalogued the roots that generate them. This desk puts the two side by side: a searchable inventory of 930 Whitney root records, the Devanagari lemmata each one matches in the archived snapshot, and exactly where every cross-reference came from. It answers “which root built this word?” without pretending the join is an etymology.

01 · where the join lands

Where the join lands

The join is deliberately uneven, and that is informative. It is strongest where Wiktionary files bare roots, and weakest where words are compounds, loans or neologisms. Each bar compares lemmata carrying a root or derivation cross-reference with all Devanagari lemmata in that category; every label opens the exact Wiktionary category.

02 · the root inventory

The root inventory

Whitney’s numbered root list, with its gloss, grammatical class, Vedic period tags, DCS frequency, attested forms and Monier-Williams / Apte record IDs. A Devanagari form appears only when that exact lemma is present in the archived Wiktionary snapshot — those links are guaranteed real. The other rows are quoted from the crosswalk and link to the source file, not to an invented Wiktionary page.

03 · how the join was made

How the join was made

No step guesses a reading. Two provable bridges connect a Devanagari Wiktionary title to SLP1-keyed scholarly data; the whole input record is archived so the join can be replayed offline.
